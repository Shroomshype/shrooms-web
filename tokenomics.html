How value moves

Four steps, and the only external input is people trading SHROOMS. No team wallet funds the rewards, because there is no team wallet.

01

Someone trades SHROOMS

Montra charges a fee on every trade and routes it, in HYPE, to the Liquidity Spores treasury.

02

The treasury drips

2% of the reserve moves into backing each week and mints the matching SPORES. The rest waits.

03

Farmers earn it

That SPORES streams to everyone staking SHROOMS, split by strain weight and multiplier.

04

They redeem or burn

Redeeming returns HYPE at the floor. Burning for boosts leaves the HYPE behind, lifting the floor for everyone else.

Where the money comes from

Every reward paid in this protocol started as someone trading SHROOMS. Montra charges 1% on every trade, and half of that goes to the treasury — so every 200 HYPE of volume puts 1 HYPE behind SPORES. The other half funds the team and marketing. Week one's modelled volume is about —. The launch spike fades, but organic trading does not stop, and that baseline is what makes the whole thing perpetual.

Where the 1% goes. 50% to the Liquidity Spores treasury, which backs every SPORES and funds every reward. 50% to team and marketing. Montra's fee route is public on-chain config, so this is verifiable rather than a claim — and every figure on this page is modelled on the treasury's half, not the full fee.

Why we don't spend it all at the party

Fees arrive front-loaded. Paying them out as they land burns the entire budget in the first month. Holding them in a reserve and releasing 2% a week turns a spike into an income — and because organic trading keeps topping the reserve up, it settles at an equilibrium around — rather than draining away.

    SPORES supply

    Supply is an output, not a policy. It rises when fees arrive and falls when players burn, and it is capped at one billion. Reaching the cap would not be an ending: past it, incoming HYPE can mint nothing, so every HYPE becomes pure backing and the floor climbs faster.

    Circulating SPORES against the cap

    Log scale. Under this fee model supply stays well below the ceiling — the cap is real, but fee income is the binding constraint, not the cap.

    Multipliers

    Boosts change your share of a fixed pot. They cannot mint SPORES, so however they are tuned the game layer can never inflate the token — that safety comes free from funding rewards with fees instead of emissions.

    What a position can reach

    Fertiliser covers a quantity of stake, not a percentage. Stake more and the new stake is bare until you fertilise it too.

    The long run

    Most farm tokens die the same way: emissions outrun demand, price falls, yields look worse, stakers leave, and it unwinds. Shrooms cannot fail that way, because there are no emissions to outrun anything. It can only run quiet — and quiet still pays.

    If trading grows

    The reserve builds faster than it releases, drips get larger, and every burn compounds the floor. The flywheel is simply volume.

    If trading stays flat

    The reserve finds equilibrium where weekly fees equal the 2% release, and rewards become a standing income rather than a countdown.

    If trading stops

    Minting stops, the reserve pays out slowly, and every SPORES stays redeemable against a treasury that is still fully backed. It winds down instead of collapsing.
